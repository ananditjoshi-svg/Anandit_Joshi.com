title: "The Next Competitive Advantage Won't Be AI. It Will Be Better Judgment."
tag: "Perspective"
summary: "If everyone has access to better answers, what will actually differentiate the best decisions?"
---

For decades, competitive advantage came from access to information. Then it came from data. Today, it comes from AI's ability to generate answers at incredible speed.

But here's the question I keep coming back to: if everyone has access to better answers, what will actually differentiate the best decisions?

AI can summarize reports, analyze documents, identify patterns, and generate recommendations faster than any human. Yet organizations still struggle with difficult decisions. Not because they lack information. Not because they lack intelligence. But because good judgment involves far more than producing an answer.

It requires understanding context, weighing trade-offs, recognizing uncertainty, questioning assumptions, and knowing when confidence should give way to caution.

As AI becomes more capable, I believe one of the most important questions is no longer *"How do we build better AI?"* Instead, it may be:

> How do we help people exercise better judgment with AI?

This isn't a conclusion. It's a question I've been thinking about deeply, and one I plan to explore much more in the months ahead.
